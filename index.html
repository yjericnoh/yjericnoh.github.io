About

I am an undergraduate in Mechanical Engineering at UNIST, working on robot vision and intelligent manipulation.

Since December 2025 I have been an undergraduate researcher in the Intelligent Robotics Lab (IntRo) at UNIST, advised by Prof. Junhyoung Ha, where I work on imitation learning for manipulator pick-and-place: building the training and simulation environment, processing demonstration data, and improving the tactile encoder of the policy.

My second interest is the numerical side of simulation itself — how many degrees of freedom a deformable-rod model needs, and where along the rod they should be spent, so that a simulated contact force or tip pose is accurate enough to train and evaluate a controller against.

I will spend the Spring 2027 semester at HKUST as an exchange student, and plan to continue to graduate study in robot vision and manipulation.

Research

2026 –

Goal-oriented spatial discretization for continuum-robot simulation

Research proposal, in preparation

At a fixed degree-of-freedom budget, a discretized rod model can place its nodes to minimize shape error or to minimize the error of the quantity a task actually depends on — and the two need not agree. I formulate node placement as an output-sensitive allocation problem, using an adjoint-weighted local defect as the importance indicator, and separate mechanical resolution, collision-sampling resolution, and node placement as independent variables in a factorial study.

Kirchhoff-type rod in MuJoCo · discrete adjoint · quasi-static equilibrium by residual root-finding

Dec 2025 –

Imitation learning for manipulator pick-and-place with force feedback

Undergraduate researcher, Intelligent Robotics Lab (IntRo), UNIST

  • Built the full data and training environment: teleoperated demonstration recording, episode acceptance criteria, dataset splits held fixed across runs, and containerized GPU training.
  • Trained vision–state–tactile policies (frozen DINOv2 backbone, tactile tokenizer, action head) predicting 6-D actions, and evaluated them on sealed test episodes and in physics replay rather than on training loss alone.
  • Implemented gripper force feedback from simulated contact forces, replacing a position-error virtual coupling, and audited the recorded action channel for artifacts that were teaching the policy the wrong grasping behavior.

PyTorch · MuJoCo · ROS 2 · Docker · OpenManipulator-X

2026

Physics-informed neural networks for heat conduction

Course research, Heat Transfer, UNIST

Diagnosed loss-term imbalance in a PINN solution of a conduction problem, non-dimensionalized the governing equations to rebalance the residual and boundary terms, and compared an Adam-to-L-BFGS schedule against Adam alone. Wrote up where a PINN adds value over FDM-based inverse optimization, and where it does not.
